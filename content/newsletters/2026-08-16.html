How Spain caged France

A World Cup semi-final against the fastest counter-attack in the tournament, held to 0.26 expected goals and nothing on target. Not by pressing harder or sitting deeper — by three rules, kept for ninety minutes.

Thanos  ·  6 min read  ·  Defending
01 Keep the ball. A counter-attack needs a turnover, so the first rule is the one nobody counts as defending: don't give them one. The ball goes round through the pivot and the two eights rather than forward, because a forward whose whole value is the space in front of him is worth nothing while you have possession at your own centre-back.
02 Shut the lines. When it is finally lost, the two banks sit close enough that the corridor between them has nothing to receive. The temptation is to follow the ten dropping into the pocket — following him is what opens the gap he was looking for. Hold the line, and he takes it with his back to goal and plays it backwards. That is a successful defensive action with nobody making a tackle.
03 Double the winger. Every time, even when it looks excessive. One-against-one against genuine pace is close to a coin flip, and it is a coin flip you never have to accept. Two shirts arriving together is not a duel at all.
