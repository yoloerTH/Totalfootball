What shipped

Draw the pass.
The board plays it.

An arrow used to be a line you drew over the top of a phase you had already posed by hand. This week it became the thing that poses it. Here is everything that landed in the Studio.

The principle

The board should carry out the move you described, not just illustrate it.

Five changes

All of it is live now. Open any system you already have and it is there.

01
The arrow tools carry out the move.
Tap Pass, tap the passer, tap who receives it. The arrow is drawn on this phase and the ball is on the receiver in the next one. Run, Carry, Press and Switch do the same. Several actions land on the same next phase, because a pass, the overlap it releases and the press it beats are one beat of football, not three. Everything it poses stays hand draggable afterwards.
02
Arrows hold on to their players.
An arrow end can name a player instead of a fixed point, so moving the right back takes his overlap arrow with him. Bound ends travel through the move rather than snapping at either end of it.
03
You can adjust an arrow after you draw it.
Handles on both ends and on the bow, plus a drag on the body. Dragging the body moves only the ends that sit on grass, so repositioning a run never tears its start off the player making it.
04
A bowed arrow now bows the path.
The board used to draw the curve and then send the ball straight through it. On a phase where the bow is the whole idea, round the press or in behind the full back, the film contradicted the board it was made from. It travels the curve you drew now.
05
Move speed is a control, and slowing it relaxes the curve.
A coach wrote in to say players and the ball travel too fast, and that the only speed setting was the one between phases. Both halves of that were right. The move itself is now yours to set, and slowing it also softens the acceleration rather than stretching the same sprint over a longer clock.
